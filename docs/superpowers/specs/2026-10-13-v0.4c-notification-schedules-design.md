# Pulsix v0.4c — Notification schedules and on-call design

v0.4b connected monitoring alerts to notification channels: a route says
which alert reaches which channel. It says nothing about *when*, or about
*who is awake*. This sub-project answers both — a delivery window on a
route, and an on-call rotation that chooses the channel by the clock.

The spec is binding for the implementation plan that follows it. Where it
records a decision, that decision was taken deliberately and the
reasoning is kept, because the reasoning is what a later reader needs in
order to change it well.

## What already exists

Most of the time machinery is built and tested:

- `maintenance.Window` stores a recurrence as **cron plus duration plus
  timezone**, and `maintenance.ActiveAt(w, at)` answers "is this window
  active at this instant" — exactly the primitive a delivery window needs.
- `cronexpr` has a *simple mode* that compiles a friendly form's input to
  cron on save, which is how the schedule form already avoids showing
  anyone a cron field.
- `internal/calendar` already lays scheduled runs and maintenance windows
  over a time range, so a rotation's shifts have somewhere to be drawn.
- v0.4b's follower already holds an event it may not deliver yet and
  delivers it later: `alert_notify_state` carries `suppressed`, and the
  catch-up pass re-resolves and sends when the reason lapses.

What does not exist at all: any notion of a delivery window, and any
notion of on-call. `internal/schedules` is job timetables and
`internal/schedule/retest.go` is the daily connection re-test; neither is
this.

### Scope

Backlog 4.4 names three things — time windows, weekdays, on-call
rotation. The recommendation at design time was to cut the rotation into
its own sub-project, on the grounds that windows mostly reuse
`ActiveAt` while a rotation needs a roster, handovers and stand-ins of its
own, and that two routes with complementary windows already cover the
simple case. That was overruled: all three ship as v0.4c.

The design is nonetheless ordered so the plan can deliver the window
before the rotation. Both are in v0.4c; neither blocks the other's tests.

## Decisions

**1. The window and the channel source both hang off the route.** A route
gains two optional things: a delivery window (when may it deliver) and a
channel source (the fixed list it has today, or a rotation). A rotation is
therefore a *channel chooser*, not a schedule — two separate ideas at one
place. The route stays the single page someone opens to find out why
something did or did not arrive.

**2. The window stores when delivery is allowed, not when it is
forbidden.** Quiet hours from 22:00 to 07:00 are expressed as "deliver
07:00 to 22:00". The inverted polarity would be equivalent, but a
notification schedule is named for delivery, and every reader would
otherwise have to turn it around in their head. It is stored in
`maintenance.Window`'s own shape — cron, duration, timezone — so
`ActiveAt` applies without a translation layer.

A window that crosses midnight needs no special case and is worth saying
so: quiet hours of 22:00 to 07:00 are the cron `0 7 * * *` with a
fifteen-hour duration. The duration is what makes the window an interval
rather than an instant, and nothing in it has to respect a day boundary.

**3. An alert outside the window is held and caught up, and chosen
severities break through.** The default is to hold and deliver once the
window opens, which reuses the catch-up path v0.4b already built. On top
of that the route carries a second, smaller severity set that ignores the
window.

Quiet hours without a breakthrough are more dangerous than no quiet hours
at all: a `crit` raised at 03:00 and held until 07:00 is precisely the
case monitoring exists for. The breakthrough set is empty by default, so
a window is absolute until someone decides otherwise.

**4. The breakthrough crosses the window, never the suppression.** A
maintenance window silences a `crit` too — that is what a maintenance
window is for. If a breakthrough severity could cross it, maintenance
would stop being a promise and become a request.

**5. A rotation is a cycle plus exceptions.** An ordered channel list, a
period in whole days, an anchor and a timezone. Who is on call follows by
arithmetic, for any instant, past or future, in constant time and with no
stored shifts — so the roster never runs out and needs no generator to
refill it. Stand-ins are a small exception table on top ("from the 3rd to
the 5th, Bob instead of Anna").

**The anchor is the first handover itself** — a date *and* a time of day
in the rotation's timezone — not a date with a separate handover-time
field beside it. Two fields would be one fact stored twice, and the first
disagreement between them would have no right answer. "Mondays at 09:00,
weekly" is anchor = some Monday 09:00, period = 7; the GUI shows the
weekday and time read back off the anchor rather than storing them again.

The period is whole days because that is what the handover arithmetic in
decision 6 steps in. Shifts shorter than a day are not expressible, and
deliberately so: a rotation that hands over twice a day is a schedule
problem, not an on-call one.

Explicit stored shifts were the alternative and are easier to read and to
edit, but they end: a generator that stops leaves the on-call rota
silently empty. A cron-driven handover reuses more existing code but
cannot answer "who was on call on 3 March" without counting forward from
the anchor.

**6. Handovers are computed in calendar units, not in durations.** This is
the decision that would otherwise break twice a year. `(t − anchor) /
period` is duration arithmetic; with a seven-day period and a handover at
09:00 local, every daylight-saving change shifts that handover by an hour
— permanently, because the error accumulates. Handover instants are
therefore produced by repeated `AddDate` **in the rotation's own
timezone**, so 09:00 stays 09:00. `internal/maintenance` solves the same
problem through `cronexpr` plus `time.Location`; this follows the same
line with a period in place of an expression.

**7. Overlapping exceptions resolve by a strict total order: later start
wins; same start, later end wins; tie, the smaller id byte for byte.** No
two distinct exceptions compare equal, so folding over a slice never
depends on the order it was read in — the same discipline v0.4a's `Mute`
applies for the same reason.

Later-start-wins is the predictable rule rather than the convenient one.
Whoever enters a stand-in is thinking about a span, not about an order of
entry; "most recently created wins" feels right while typing and is a
riddle when read back a month later.

**8. The rotation is resolved at delivery time, not at event time.** An
alert raised at 03:00 and caught up at 07:00 goes to whoever is on call at
**07:00**. The person who slept through it and has since handed over can
do nothing; their replacement can. The delivery log still records which
channel was actually served, so nothing is lost for an audit.

**9. A window that can no longer be evaluated delivers.** Backlog 4.29
records the trap: a maintenance window whose cron or timezone stops
parsing goes silently inactive, and the symptom is "alerts fired during a
maintenance window" with nothing pointing at why. Here the same trap is
worse, because this window decides whether anybody learns anything at all.

Both failure directions are wrong and only one is dangerous. A broken
window that delivers is loud, so somebody notices and fixes it. A broken
window that holds is silent, and it is discovered at the next outage
nobody heard about. Validation at save time means this can only arise from
a changed timezone database or a hand-edited row; it is logged at warn
level and marked on the route in the GUI.

## Package boundary

**`internal/oncall`, new**, carries the rotation and only the rotation:

- `Rotation` — ordered channels, period in whole days, anchor (the first
  handover itself), timezone
- `Exception` — a span and a replacement channel
- `ChannelAt(rotation, exceptions, t) uuid.UUID` — pure, no database
- `Shifts(rotation, exceptions, from, to) []Shift` — the range split into
  segments with one channel each, the shape `maintenance.Occurrences`
  already has and `internal/calendar` can already draw

**`internal/alertroutes` gains two things on the route**, and nothing
else: the window (`window_cron`, `window_duration`, `window_timezone`, all
NULL for "always") and the channel source (the existing fixed list, or a
`rotation_id`). Plus the breakthrough severities as a fourth selection
table beside v0.4b's three.

The package is about 1 400 lines across eight files today, none over 400.
The window is a field and a check and fits; the rotation would not have.

## Data model

Migration `00054`, additive, with the house conventions: the
`org_isolation` / `org_isolation_bypass` policy pair, `FORCE ROW LEVEL
SECURITY`, `UNIQUE (org_id, id)`, and composite foreign keys per backlog
1.30.

- `oncall_rotations` — id, org_id, name, period (whole days), anchor (the
  first handover, date and time), timezone, revision
- `oncall_rotation_channels` — ordered, composite FK to
  `notification_channels (org_id, id)`
- `oncall_exceptions` — span, replacement channel, composite FKs
- `alert_routes` gains the window columns and `rotation_id`, the latter a
  composite FK so a deleted rotation takes the reference with it
- `alert_route_breakthrough_severities` — the fourth selection table

### A fifth ledger state

`alert_notify_state` carries `sent`, `suppressed`, `unrouted` and
`expired`. Holding for a delivery window is structurally identical to
`suppressed` — hold, re-check, send when the alert is still open — but it
is a different *why*, and v0.4b's decision 8 made "why did nothing arrive"
a queryable fact rather than an absence.

So a fifth state, `deferred`, and the catch-up pass reads both.

When an event meets both gates, `suppressed` wins: that the host is in
maintenance is the first thing an operator needs to know; the time of day
is the second. An event may move from `deferred` to `suppressed` between
ticks if a maintenance window began meanwhile, and that needs no special
case.

## The follower's decision

Per event: resolve the matching routes, resolve their channels (now
possibly through a rotation), check suppression, check the window, record,
then one batch per channel.

Two things deliberately do **not** change, which is why this section is
short. Batching stays per channel, because `now` is fixed within a tick
and the rotation's answer is therefore deterministic for that tick. And
the batch key stays the hash over the sorted event ids — a caught-up event
joining freshly decided ones simply makes a different set and so a
different key.

A rotation that resolves to no channel — every exception overlapping, or
the list emptied — records `unrouted` and shows the same "reaches nobody"
indicator v0.4b built for a channel-less route. Nothing here goes quietly
inactive.

## API and permissions

- `GET` / `POST` `/orgs/{org}/oncall-rotations`
- `GET` / `PATCH` / `DELETE` `/orgs/{org}/oncall-rotations/{id}` — `PATCH`
  requires `If-Match`, per backlog 1.31
- `GET` / `POST` `/orgs/{org}/oncall-rotations/{id}/exceptions`,
  `DELETE` `.../exceptions/{id}`
- `GET` `/orgs/{org}/oncall-rotations/{id}/shifts?from=&to=`

The delivery window gets **no endpoints of its own**: it is fields on the
route and travels over the existing `alert-routes` routes. So does the
channel source.

Permissions stay `PermChannelsRead` / `PermChannelsWrite`. A rotation
decides where alerts leave the system, which is the same reasoning v0.4b
used for routes, and so the same bar.

## GUI

Everything in `features/notifications`, beside channels and routes.

`RouteDialog` gains two sections. The **delivery window** as weekday
checkboxes, two times and a timezone — no cron field; `cronexpr`'s simple
mode compiles it on save, exactly as the schedule form already does.
Beside it the **breakthrough severities** as a second, smaller severity
selection, shown only when a window is set, labelled so it carries its own
meaning: "deliver these severities outside the window too". And the
**channel source** as a toggle between the fixed list and a rotation.

A `RotationsPage` with the channel list in its order, the period, anchor
and the handover weekday and time read back off it, and a **roster strip**
showing who is on call now and
over the coming weeks, with stand-ins marked.

### The hold indicator

v0.4b made "why did nothing arrive" a fact in the ledger, but one
reachable only by SQL. With quiet hours it becomes an everyday question:
*the alert has been open since three — why did nobody wake me?* The alert
detail answers it — "notification held until 07:00", or until the
maintenance window ends. A small addition to `GET /alerts/{id}`, and the
difference between a system people trust and one they work around.

## Error handling

Decision 9 in practice, and deletion handled by foreign keys rather than
by application discipline:

- A window that cannot be evaluated delivers, logs at warn level, and is
  marked on the route.
- A deleted channel leaves the rotation's list and any exception naming
  it. If that empties the list, the follower records `unrouted` and the
  route shows "reaches nobody".
- A deleted rotation takes the route's channel source with it — the same
  indicator, no new mechanism.

## Testing

Failing test first, per the house rules.

- `ChannelAt`, table-driven, **across a daylight-saving change** — the
  case that would otherwise break twice a year, and the only reason
  decision 6 exists
- The exception order: no two distinct exceptions compare equal, and
  folding is independent of visit order
- `Shifts` over a range, including an exception that splits a shift
- Four obligatory cases at the follower: a `deferred` event is delivered
  once the window opens; a breakthrough severity ignores the window; a
  maintenance window silences a breakthrough severity **too** (decision
  4); and the rotation is resolved at delivery time, not event time
  (decision 8)
- The API through `apitest`, the GUI with Vitest, one Playwright pass

The coverage floor for `internal/oncall` is 80 %, **measured under Go
1.27.2**. 1.27.1 gives every coverage block the statement count of its
enclosing function, so a floor set on its numbers is not a floor —
backlog 1.36, and v0.4b's own `internal/api/apitest` surprise, both say so.

## Explicitly not in this sub-project

- Escalation chains (v0.4d, backlog 4.9)
- The notification bell and a per-user inbox (v0.4e, backlog 1.12a)
- Native Telegram, Slack, Mattermost, ntfy, Pushover, Twilio, PagerDuty
  channels (backlog 4.7, 4.8) — additive on `notify.Kind`, one bounded
  task each

  Pulsix has no notion of a person's own address, and inventing one here
  would commit the shape v0.4e's inbox has to live with.

## As built

Everything above shipped as designed, with one narrowing decision 3's own
words do not make, and two defects this milestone found in code already on
`main`.

### The window gate is per route, not per event

Decision 3 says an alert outside the window "is held and caught up",
without qualifying which routes that covers. What shipped holds it **per
route**: `resolution.route` (`internal/alertroutes/follow_resolve.go`)
applies the window gate to each matching route on its own and unions the
survivors, rather than gating the union. When a crit alert matches two
routes — say a 24/7 route and a weekday-only route — and the 24/7 route's
window is open while the weekday route's is shut, the 24/7 route delivers
and the event is recorded `sent`. The weekday route never hears about that
event, not even once its own window opens later. There is no `deferred`
row waiting for it, because there is nothing left to defer: the ledger
holds one row per *event*, not one per `(event, route)` pair, and `sent`
is the event's only recorded outcome.

The alternative was considered and rejected, not overlooked.  Gating the
union — holding the whole event until every matching route's window is
open — would re-enqueue the 24/7 route's channel on every tick of the
holding period, since that route's own window was never shut in the first
place; a route an operator believes has already heard about an alert would
be told about it again, repeatedly, for as long as the slower route stays
closed. Splitting the ledger to track partial delivery — recording
`deferred` for the event while one route has already delivered it — would
make `alert_notify_state` lie about the one question it exists to answer:
a reader of "why did nothing arrive" would see `deferred` and conclude
nobody was told, when somebody was. v0.4b's own decision 8 — a route (and,
through it, the ledger) must say plainly when something did not reach
anybody, never leave that ambiguous — is what rules that shape out.

`TestAnOpenRouteDeliversWhileAClosedOneWaitsForNobody`
(`internal/alertroutes/follow_window_test.go`) pins the chosen behaviour at
the unit level; `TestOncallEndToEnd` (`internal/e2e/oncall_test.go`) is its
end-to-end twin for the rotation half of the same gate.

The cost is real and worth naming plainly: an organization running a
24/7 route alongside a weekday-hours team route loses the weekday route's
own catch-up for anything the 24/7 route already carried. The weekday
team finds out about a Saturday `crit` only if they think to look, not
because the system holds it for them until Monday morning — the 24/7
route having already been told is what stands in the way. Routes add up
for delivery (decision in the v0.4b spec), but they do not add up for
*holding*: the most permissive matching window decides whether an event is
ever held at all.

### Two bugs this milestone found in code already on `main`

**A `WITH`-based DELETE-then-INSERT that reproducibly lost data on edit.**
Migration 00054's first cut of `ReplaceRotationChannels` combined the
delete and the re-insert of a rotation's channel cycle as two
data-modifying sub-statements of one `WITH` query, with the `INSERT` never
reading the `DELETE`'s own output. Postgres documents the relative order of
such sub-statements as unspecified, and `oncall_rotation_channels`' own
primary key — `(rotation_id, position)` — turned that into a reproducible
failure: a cycle's new channel list almost always reuses position 0, the
same key the old list's position-0 row already held, so every update that
kept any channel at the same position hit a duplicate-key violation. Caught
in-branch (`TestStoreUpdateReplacesChannelsAndRotationFields`) before it
ever reached `main`, and fixed by splitting the one statement into
`DeleteRotationChannels` followed by `InsertRotationChannels`, run in
sequence on the same transaction (`internal/oncall/store_sql.go`,
`replaceChannels`).

The identical shape was already sitting on `main`, unnoticed, in
`internal/alertroutes`' own route selections —
`alert_route_severities`, `alert_route_events` and `alert_route_channels`
— each replaced through the same single-`WITH` DELETE-then-INSERT pattern,
and a fourth table this milestone added
(`alert_route_breakthrough_severities`) was written the same way by
imitation. There the symptom was worse than a thrown error: those tables'
`INSERT`s use `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, so when the planner ran the
`DELETE` after the `INSERT` instead of before it, the already-present row
the `INSERT` had just (correctly) left untouched was then deleted anyway —
a row present both before and after an edit simply vanished, with no
error at all. Reproduced directly against the original statement text,
outside any Go caller, 10 times out of 10: seeding severities
`[crit, warn]` and then replacing with `[crit]` left the table empty every
time, not just missing `warn`. Fixed the same way — `Delete*`/`Insert*`
pairs run as two statements rather than one `WITH`, for all four
tables — once `TestStoreUpdateReplacesBreakthroughOverAnExistingRow` caught
the new table failing and the review that followed went looking for the
same shape in its three siblings.

**`Validate` required the fixed channel list unconditionally, so a
rotation-only route could not be saved.** The route's own `Validate`
called `checkSet` on `Channels` regardless of whether `RotationID` was
set, rejecting a route that named a rotation and no fixed channels — even
though `channelsOf` (`follow_resolve.go`) reads the rotation and never
falls back to `Channels` for one that does. That made the entire on-call
half of this milestone unreachable through the API: the route editor's
own channel-source toggle could only ever compose a request the server
refused. `validateChannelSource` now enforces decision 1 directly —
exactly one channel source, a fixed list or a rotation, never neither
(reaches nobody) and never both (the rotation wins silently, so a fixed
list saved alongside one would do nothing) — caught by a reviewer against
this milestone's own GUI task and pinned by `TestValidateChannelSource`
(`internal/alertroutes/route_test.go`).
